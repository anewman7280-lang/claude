What Does Florida Medicaid Cover for Assisted Living? A Guide for Families

If you are weighing the cost of care for a parent or spouse, one question comes up quickly: does Florida Medicaid cover assisted living? The answer is yes, in part, but how it works surprises many families. Florida Medicaid does not simply pay a facility's monthly bill. Instead, eligible seniors receive help with the cost of care services through a managed long-term care program. Understanding the details early can help families in Sarasota and across Florida plan with confidence.

How Medicaid Helps With Assisted Living in Florida

In Florida, long-term care coverage for seniors is delivered through the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care (SMMC LTC) program. Eligible individuals enroll in a managed care plan that arranges and pays for covered services, which can include assisted living when it is the appropriate setting.

The program is overseen by the Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA), while the Florida Department of Elder Affairs (DOEA) helps determine medical need, and the Florida Department of Children and Families (DCF) determines financial eligibility.

Several organizations play a role:

  • The Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC): Your local ADRC is the front door for long-term care screening and the waitlist. The Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 can connect you to the right one for Sarasota County.
  • CARES (Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services): Nurses and social workers assess whether a person needs a nursing-facility level of care.
  • Managed care plans: Once enrolled, a plan and a case manager coordinate services.

What Is Typically Covered, and What Is Not

This is where families most often have questions. In general, Medicaid assistance with assisted living focuses on the care itself rather than housing costs.

Services that may be covered

  • Personal care help, such as bathing, dressing, and grooming
  • Assistance with meals and medication
  • Case management and care coordination
  • Certain therapies and other home- and community-based services

Costs that usually remain the resident's responsibility

  • Room and board. Medicaid generally does not pay for rent or meals in an assisted living setting. Residents typically pay this from income such as Social Security or a pension.
  • Personal items, outings, and extras beyond covered services

Florida's Optional State Supplementation (OSS) program may provide a small monthly payment to eligible low-income residents of licensed facilities, which can help with room and board. Ask about it during your application.

Also keep in mind that not every assisted living community participates in Medicaid. Always ask whether a facility accepts a Medicaid managed care plan and has an available Medicaid-supported space before you fall in love with a community.

Who Qualifies?

To qualify for the long-term care program, a person generally must meet three types of requirements:

  • Age or disability: Typically age 65 or older, or age 18 and older with a qualifying disability.
  • Medical need: A CARES assessment must find that the person needs a nursing-facility level of care.
  • Financial limits: Income and countable assets must fall within program limits. These limits are updated periodically, so check current figures with DCF or the Florida Medicaid resources on MyFlorida.com rather than relying on an old number.

Some assets, such as a primary home (within limits) and one vehicle, may be treated as exempt. Married couples have additional protections designed to keep the spouse at home from losing everything. Because the rules are detailed, many families benefit from speaking with a certified elder law attorney or Medicaid planner, especially before transferring or spending assets. Transfers made for less than fair value can trigger a penalty period.

How to Get Started

The process can take time, so begin as early as you can. Here is a practical roadmap:

  1. Contact your local ADRC. Request a screening for long-term care services and ask about the waitlist for the program.
  2. Gather documents. Collect identification, proof of income, bank statements, insurance cards, property records, and any legal documents such as a power of attorney.
  3. Apply for financial eligibility. You can apply through the ACCESS Florida system on the DCF website, by phone, or in person at a DCF service center.
  4. Complete the CARES assessment. Be candid about every difficulty with daily activities so the assessment reflects true needs.
  5. Choose a managed care plan. Confirm your preferred assisted living community is in the plan's network.
  6. Keep records and respond promptly. Missed deadlines and paperwork requests are a common cause of delays or denials.

If an application is denied, you have the right to appeal. Do not give up without exploring that option.

Planning Ahead Makes a Difference

Many families find that Medicaid becomes part of a bigger plan that might also include Social Security, long-term care insurance, or veterans benefits. Talking through these options early, ideally before a crisis, gives you more choices and less stress. Official sources such as AHCA, DOEA, and DCF are the best places to confirm current rules, since programs and limits change.
